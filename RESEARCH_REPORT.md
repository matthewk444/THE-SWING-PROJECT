# Build swing intelligence that coaches trust

Research and implementation report for Matthew Kim • October 22, 2026

**THE SWING PROJECT is feasible as a video review and hitter-development assistant, with a useful first version built around pretrained body tracking, carefully defined observations, and coach review.** The difficult part is establishing that measurements are accurate and that a recommended change helps this hitter. Tonight's work establishes the research direction and an original local application scaffold in `/Users/matthewkim/THE SWING PROJECT`. It accepts a swing clip, estimates body landmarks, supports manual event selection, and produces limited camera-plane measurements with review prompts. It is not a trained baseball coaching model, and no player video or scientific validation has been assumed. The strongest next investment is a small consented capture program with coaches and reference measurements. A defensible product should help hitters run better practice experiments and help scouts inspect comparable evidence over time.

## Start with observable movement and player-specific comparisons

A single phone view can support frame review, visible joint tracking, and comparisons under a consistent recording setup. MediaPipe's Pose Landmarker returns **33 body landmarks**, normalized coordinates, model scores and estimated world coordinates. Its Python video API supports sequential decoded frames with timestamps, making it a practical baseline for a local OpenCV pipeline. The model is intended for general body tracking; its model card warns about occlusion, movement, lighting and depth limitations. Coordinates expressed in meters do not establish calibrated baseball biomechanics. ([MediaPipe overview](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker), [Python API](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker/python), [BlazePose model card](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-assets/Model%20Card%20BlazePose%20GHUM%203D.pdf))

The first useful observations are projected lead-knee angles at coach-confirmed events and horizontal nose movement relative to a fixed starting body reference. The nose is a visible head-position proxy. Geometry must use pixel coordinates because normalized image x and y have different scales on rectangular frames. A stationary camera and consistent view matter as much as the algorithm. Record one hitter with the full body visible, good light, explicit handedness, and an unmirrored clip. Prefer original high-frame-rate footage where available, while preserving the distinction between acquisition speed and slow-motion playback speed. A skeleton overlay must be inspectable so the coach can spot tracking failures.

**Body tracking alone cannot measure bat speed, ball exit velocity, attack angle, true 3D hip–shoulder separation, forces, or professional potential.** These require additional observations and validation. A wrist-speed peak is not proof of contact. Manual event labels are preferable for the first version, with uncertain contact kept uncertain. Projected angles depend on viewpoint; successful hitters need not share one ideal swing. Research also shows that pitch knowledge changes batting timing, so tee, machine and live-pitch swings cannot be pooled as equivalent tasks. ([Kidokoro et al., 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0230385))

Feedback should connect an observation to frame evidence, identify its limitations, and propose one coach-reviewed experiment. For example: compare five similar swings, inspect movement variability alongside contact quality, try one cue, then repeat with the same setup. An LLM can eventually explain structured results, but measurement should remain deterministic and auditable. Initial scouting value comes from organized clips, longitudinal comparisons and transparent notes. A universal mechanics score or MLB projection would overstate what this evidence can support.

## Public datasets help research but leave the core data gap open

Baseball Savant provides valuable MLB context. Its bat-tracking leaderboard covers data beginning in the second half of 2023 and distinguishes sweet-spot swing speed from 3D bat-head travel. The CSV dictionary includes pitch context and fields such as exit velocity, launch angle, attack angle and swing-path tilt. These describe different quantities; a video-derived body angle is not interchangeable with a Statcast bat measurement. The MIT-licensed `pybaseball` package offers a convenient future ingestion route. ([Savant leaderboard](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/leaderboard/bat-tracking), [CSV dictionary](https://baseballsavant.mlb.com/csv-docs), [pybaseball](https://github.com/jldbc/pybaseball))

Driveline's OpenBiomechanics Project is particularly relevant: its datasheet reports **677 hitting trials from 98 athletes**, including **604 paired HitTrax records**, predominantly collegiate participants aged 17–31. It offers motion-capture signals, metadata and point-of-interest tables. That makes it useful for studying definitions, variability and candidate relationships. It does not establish youth norms or a universal intervention policy, and repeated trials from one athlete are not independent people. ([OBP datasheet](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/DATASHEET.md), [Hitting documentation](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/baseball_hitting/README.md))

Other resources address different problems. MLB-YouTube contains 20 postseason games, over 42 hours of footage and 4,290 activity clips, including 2,506 swing examples. Its action/result labels do not diagnose mechanics. BaseballCV offers detection resources and lists 50 unannotated amateur batting-practice videos. Those could support detection experiments, but unannotated footage cannot directly teach corrective coaching. **The reviewed sources did not supply a large, clearly commercially licensed dataset combining consumer-camera swings, reference biomechanics, expert coaching labels and longitudinal outcomes.** ([MLB-YouTube](https://github.com/piergiaj/mlb-youtube), [BaseballCV](https://github.com/BaseballCV/BaseballCV))

Build that missing evidence incrementally. Record consent, permitted analysis/training uses, athlete ID, session, handedness, camera location, acquisition frame rate, drill, bat dimensions, pitch context where known, annotated events and external outcomes. Begin with adults and separate identity from motion records. A practical first workflow cohort is 10–20 hitters; a broader measurement pilot can expand to 20–40 with repeated sessions, both batting sides and varied devices. These are planning estimates, not sufficient sample sizes for proving coaching effectiveness. Split future model evaluation by athlete and session so adjacent frames or similar swings do not leak into both training and testing.

## Choose open source components with separate rights checks

MediaPipe is the recommended initial pose engine because its official Tasks API provides a direct path from decoded frames to landmarks. Its repository uses Apache 2.0. MMPose, also Apache 2.0, is a later alternative for custom datasets, training and pose-model benchmarking. It adds complexity, so the decision should follow measured failure cases on real swings. The initial application should retain a replaceable pose interface so model choice does not dictate the rest of the product. ([MediaPipe license](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/LICENSE), [MMPose](https://github.com/open-mmlab/mmpose))

Existing baseball demonstrations offer design lessons. One inspected capstone documents contact proxies, missing bat/ball tracking, slow-motion timing errors and normalization problems; its inspected metadata did not establish a license. We can learn from these stated limitations without copying its code. Ultralytics uses AGPL-3.0, and BaseballCV describes community AGPL and commercial licensing, so neither should silently become a dependency of a proprietary scouting service without reviewing the relevant terms. ([Capstone](https://github.com/andrew9990828/baseball-swing-analysis-capstone), [Ultralytics license](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/blob/main/LICENSE), [BaseballCV licensing](https://github.com/BaseballCV/BaseballCV#-license))

**Code permission is separate from model, dataset and footage permission.** OBP code is MIT-licensed, but its data and biomechanics documentation use CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 with additional stated conditions affecting professional-sports and financial-analysis affiliations and future profit intent. Such use requires addressing its written paid-license requirements. Public MLB access also does not confer blanket redistribution or commercial rights. Keep a source manifest and obtain project-specific rights advice or permissions before commercial/team deployment. Do not bundle restricted datasets, broadcast clips or derivative trained artifacts on the assumption that a public download grants every intended use. ([OBP data terms](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/LICENSE-DATA.md), [OBP code terms](https://github.com/drivelineresearch/openbiomechanics/blob/main/LICENSE-CODE.md), [MLB terms](https://www.mlb.com/official-information/terms-of-use?affiliate_id=false))

OpenCap is a promising later research option for calibrated multicamera analysis. Its published validation used two or more smartphones and reported 4.5° mean joint-angle absolute error in ten participants performing walking, squats, chair rise and drop jumps. **That is not batting accuracy.** Swing-specific validation, capture requirements and service/commercial terms would still need investigation. ([OpenCap original paper](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011462), [OpenCap core](https://github.com/opencap-org/opencap-core))

## Validate measurement before claiming better hitting

Four questions need separate answers: does the system measure correctly, repeat reliably, produce useful observations, and improve player outcomes? Unit tests address software behavior, not these scientific claims. Have two trained reviewers independently mark visible landmarks and event windows, adjudicate disagreement, and retain annotation uncertainty. Compare 2D extraction to same-view manual labels using normalized landmark error, angle error and event error in frames or milliseconds when acquisition timing is established. Report coverage and abstention as well as accuracy; a model that declines difficult clips is different from one that silently invents confident results.

For true 3D or bat metrics, obtain synchronized calibrated cameras or reference motion capture and independent bat/ball instrumentation. Report bias, mean absolute error, limits of agreement, test–retest variation and results by athlete/device/lighting conditions. Correlation alone is insufficient: two systems can correlate while disagreeing systematically. Release thresholds should be chosen after measuring pilot error and the smallest useful change. The pitching-focused pitchAI validation illustrates reference comparison using time-series error and Bland–Altman analysis; it supplies a study-design example, not validation for this batting application. ([pitchAI validation](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14763141.2022.2137425))

Next, ask coaches to judge visible evidence and actionability without seeing an algorithmic grade first. Finally, test coach-plus-tool against usual coaching prospectively using prespecified outcomes, baseline/follow-up and comparable pitch tasks. Swing metrics and hitting results are confounded by timing, pitch recognition and pitch location; an observed association is not evidence that changing a joint angle will improve contact. A recent research preprint explicitly examines these timing and context problems. ([Powers and Yurko, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01238))

Meaningful differentiation should therefore center on **traceable observations, honest uncertainty, consistent longitudinal capture and coach-controlled experiments**. This is a proposed product strategy, not a claim that competitors lack these features; no exhaustive commercial-product comparison was completed. The durable asset would be consented, diverse footage linked to trusted labels and outcomes, plus a workflow that coaches choose to use repeatedly.

## Tonight establishes the scaffold; the pilot determines the product

The project now contains an original Streamlit interface and a separate analysis module. Written functionality includes local MP4/MOV/AVI upload, clip-size limits, sequential MediaPipe video inference, a selected-frame skeleton overlay, right/left lead-leg mapping, manual review/plant/contact frame selection, projected lead-knee angles, horizontal nose range normalized by starting projected torso length, low-score/missing-landmark guards, review prompts, and downloadable review/landmark JSON. Videos are processed using temporary files; results remain in session memory until cleared. The app does not intentionally upload footage or call an LLM, although MediaPipe's package notice describes performance/utilization telemetry. ([MediaPipe package notice](https://pypi.org/project/mediapipe/))

## The next milestone is trusted evidence from real swings

The most valuable next deliverable is a small set of consented, consistently recorded swings for which a coach can agree both on what happened and whether the system represented it accurately. That evidence will reveal which measurements deserve further development and which should be removed. It also provides a concrete basis for deciding whether custom models are necessary.

Success should first mean that a hitter and coach can inspect a change, understand the uncertainty, and run a better practice experiment. Earning that trust creates a foundation for useful scouting records and eventually stronger automated advice. The research does not justify an autonomous expert coach today; it does justify building and testing this narrower, practical product now.
